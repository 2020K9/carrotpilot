## K9LaneAssist (en, 0ae1911f)

Single choice of 0 or 1 in the settings screen (2026-10-25 lane menu merge: the old `K9LaneFix`, `K9LRView`, `K9OneLaneHold` and `K9LaneCenter` are this one switch now; if any old value was on, it is moved to 1 once on the first start after the update; marker `K9LaneVer`). `0` (default) off: identical to all four old features off (steering CAN bit-identical, checked by log replay). `1` on: the four features below together. Strength: `K9LaneCenterGain` (centre hold strength %), left/right target: the existing `PathOffset` (lane deviation correction, cm, up to ±30 used), applied within 0.1 s while driving. The external HUD driving view shows above the TPMS car (right above the SR / SAD line) `OFS` (offset from the lane centre in cm, + right, `--` when the lines are weak) and `LC` (hold: `off`, `--` standby, number = lateral accel it adds now in m/s², + right). (1) Lane-line post-processing (old `K9LRView` 1): modeld smooths the four lane lines per point with a Kalman filter driven by the model std. Before each update the previous lane lines are moved by the car's own speed and yaw rate taken from this frame's model output (ego-motion prediction), which reduces the lag on curve entry and bending lanes. Near points are smoothed more; far points, high yaw rate and large disagreements less; lane-line probability is averaged over about 0.15 s. A lane line that jumps more than 1 m in one frame (lane change etc.) is reset to the raw model value at once. Lane height, road edges, the model path and safety limits are unchanged; no extra GPU work and negligible CPU. Everything that reads lane lines (lane mode, lane-change logic, UI) sees the smoothed lines. In the video-replay validation set flicker dropped by about 61% vs raw; remaining: curve-entry lag about 1.3 frames (about 0.06 s), probability changes about 0.1-0.15 s later, and about 0.03 s extra lag on a lane line under the car. Applied within 5 s; switching off and on again restarts the filter. Checked only by low-resolution recorded-video replay simulation and device-log replay; not vehicle-validated. (2) Lane-mode drift fix (old `K9LaneFix` 1 F1): (1) the lane-mode path starts at the car (model path start) and merges into the lane-line path over 1 s; a large initial offset extends the merge up to 2 s, but only as far as the merge curvature stays small, so at high speed it stays 1 s; when lane mode just turns on the correction is ramped in over 0.5 s. While both lane lines are clear it measures the gap car left edge <-> left line and car right edge <-> right line (K9 width 1.915 m, filtered) and remembers the centre target gap (lane width / 2 - car width / 2) and the minimum gap (smaller of the two, at least 0.3 m). When only one line is visible afterwards, it adds a small steering curvature so the gap to that line returns to the remembered centre gap, and pushes harder when closer than the minimum gap. Two lines or none: nothing is added (laneless as before). It also drops the memory at once when the visible line moves by itself, i.e. not explained by the car's own motion (previous frame's line moved along the model path), by more than 0.12 m summed over about 1.5 s (merge or branch line), and the push target then drops to 0 at once (the output falls at the jerk limit); the push already fades once that motion exceeds 0.03 m, and nothing is pushed in the first 0.6 s after only one line remains (a line dropout of 0.3 s or less does not restart that wait). While the model path itself turns quickly (lateral jerk above 0.1-0.3 m/s^3) the hold adds no jerk in the same direction (a push cut by the watch still falls). The damping (line rate) only takes back the lateral speed the hold itself added, so a jumpy line estimate never makes it push away from the centre target. The added amount is at most 0.25 m/s^2 lateral (0.6 inside the minimum gap), changes at most 0.15 m/s^3, with a 1 s ramp in/out; while ramping out the push only shrinks and never changes direction. A visible line with probability below 0.9 pushes proportionally less (0 at 0.45). Not used in lane mode. Checked only by device-log replay and closed-loop simulation; not vehicle-validated. (4) Lane centre hold (old `K9LaneCenter` 1): works only in laneless (the car follows the model path). While both lane lines are clear (probability 0.7 or more, no jitter or jump), the lane width is plausible (2.8-4.2 m) and there is no width change ahead, width jump or a line moving away (merge, split) for 1 s, it measures how far the car is from the lane centre (right +, filtered). Beyond 5 cm it adds a small steering curvature back toward the centre (proportional to the offset plus a slow integral that removes a steady bias; the damping only takes back the lateral speed this function added, so a jumpy lane estimate never pushes away from the centre). The added amount is at most 0.2 m/s^2 lateral, changes at most 0.15 m/s^3, with a 1 s ramp in/out; while ramping out it only shrinks. Not active below 1 km/h, in turns (fades out between 0.8 and 1.5 m/s^2 model lateral accel), in lane change, with the blinker or driver steering, or in lane mode. Background (2026-10-25 drives 1c9/1ca): the controller follows the model curvature independent of road roll, but the laneless model pulls back to the centre only weakly (about 1e-3 1/m curvature per m of offset), so a small curvature bias left the car 15-20 cm right of centre; this adds the missing pull. At low speed a curvature cap of 2.0e-3 1/m (about 4.6 deg steering wheel) and a rate cap of 4.0e-3 1/m/s (about 9 deg/s) are added to the lateral accel / jerk limits so the wheel never jerks or shakes; the slow integral learns only above 11 km/h.

## K9LaneCenterGain (en, 0ae1911f)

How hard `K9LaneAssist`=1 pulls toward the lane centre (both-line hold and one-line gap hold). 0-200 %, 10 % steps, `100` (default) = validated strength (`K9LaneAssist` 1 as is), `0` = no pull, `200` = twice. The lateral accel, jerk, curvature and rate limits stay, so a higher value never pushes beyond them. Applied within 0.1 s while driving. Not vehicle-validated.
